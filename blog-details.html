The art of
handmade dim sum.

A closer look at the folds, steam and small gestures behind our most beloved ritual.

Words by Mei Chen
Dim Sum Specialist · 5 min read

There is a particular quiet in the Jade Wok kitchen just before the first bamboo baskets go into the steamers.

It is not silence. There is the shuffle of trays, the low conversation of the prep team and the gentle tap of a knife against the board. But the energy changes. Hands become more precise. Every fold starts to matter.

Small gestures, held in place

Dim sum asks for attention in a different way to wok cooking. The heat is still essential, but the final character is built earlier: in the softness of a wrapper, the balance of a filling, the tension in a pleat that keeps the broth where it belongs.

“The fold is not decoration. It is a promise that the filling will arrive exactly as it should.”— Mei Chen
Morning mise en place: every filling begins with texture.

The table is part of the recipe

At Jade Wok, we serve dim sum for the middle of the table. One basket is an introduction; a few baskets become a conversation. The joy is in ordering widely, passing things around and discovering which small parcel has become everyone’s favourite.

That is why we keep the menu in motion. A new vegetable, a different broth, a seasonal shellfish: the form stays familiar while the kitchen keeps listening.
